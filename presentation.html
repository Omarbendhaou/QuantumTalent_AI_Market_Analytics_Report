02 / Seasonality & Timing Slide 03 of 06

Don't wait until summer: hiring peaks between April and July

Hiring is not evenly spread across the twelve months. Demand builds in the spring, surges through early summer, and then drops off sharply after July.

Q1
The Prep Period
Demand is moderate in January and dips in February/March. Best time to get headcount approved and job descriptions locked in.
Q2
The Spring Surge
Job postings climb aggressively from April through June. Candidate availability and market movement are at their highest.
Q3
Mid-Year Apex
July hits the yearly peak with over 1,800 monthly roles, followed by an immediate sharp decline in August as vacations begin.
Q4
The Cooldown
Hiring slows down from August through October. Shift your focus to onboarding, retention, and setting up next year's pipeline.
Practical takeaway
If you start your recruiting campaign in June or July, you're competing at peak volume. Start reaching out in March and April so offers are out before candidate fatigue sets in.

05 / Summary & Next Steps Slide 06 of 06

Four simple rules for your next AI hire

Hiring doesn't have to be guesswork. Based on the data, here is the straightforward playbook for organizations planning their 2026 talent pipeline.

Focus Area What to do Why it works
1. Hiring Window Open requisitions in early spring (March/April); hire before the July peak. Avoids candidate exhaustion and beats late-season bidding wars.
2. Sourcing Strategy Look at European hubs (Germany, France) and hire based on skills, not just industry tenure. Engineers from retail or fintech transition quickly into healthcare or automotive.
3. Internal Growth Train your existing software and backend engineers in machine learning deployment. Cuts recruitment agency fees and builds loyalty with people who already know your code.
4. The Perk Advantage Offer flexible working hours, home-office stipends, and learning budgets. Out-attracts large competitors without matching their unsustainable cash salaries.
